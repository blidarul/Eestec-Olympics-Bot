# Kit changelog

Changes to the participant kit after it was first handed out. Check this when something looks different.

- **The Practice page and its limits** (README "Getting started"). On the arena site you can run an image you pushed against our three bots, against itself, or play it yourself in the browser. Limits: one run at a time per team, 10 runs per team per hour, 10 runs at once on the whole arena.
- **You may test with your own key** (README "The model"). On your laptop you are welcome to use your own Haiku key, or anything else, to experiment. In the arena your bot always plays with the key the arena sends it, on Haiku through our gateway (Candidate Rulebook, section 7).
- **The final is for the top 8 of the round robin** (README "Tournaments", "Submitting", "Schedule"; Candidate Rulebook 4.6, 10.8, 11.5). The 8 teams at the top of the round-robin table play T3, instead of the 8 best in the standings after T2. The finalists are announced when the round robin ends on Sunday morning, before T2 is played. If the round robin cannot be completed, the standings after T1 and T2 decide.
- **Live stage draw: six slots per match, T3 prep only the drawn games** (README "Tournaments", "Your bot", "Schedule"; PROTOCOL.md "Prep"; Candidate Rulebook 1.1, 1.3, 4.1, 4.4, 4.6, 10.2, 10.3). Before each round the organizers draw **six game slots for every match** on the big screen: three regular games and up to three sudden-death games (game + variant). T1/T2 still prepare the whole pool (4/8 games). **T3 prepares only the unique games in your match's draw**, so a finalist gets at most six specialist containers per match instead of 21. A prepared container is reused if a later match draws the same game. Prep remains 10 minutes per container, 16 at a time; worst case T3 is now about 30 minutes for all quarter-finals (first QF after about 10). The round robin is unchanged (12 games, T2's 8 warm + T1's 4).
- **Final format, part 2: pools, round robin, practice** (README "Tournaments", "How the automated 45 points are computed", "Getting started", "Schedule"; PROTOCOL.md "Prep"; Candidate Rulebook 1.3, 1.4, 4.4, 4.6, 10.2, 11.1, 14.8). **Pools:** the organizers choose the games of T1 (4) and T2 (8 different games), none reserved for the final; which games is not announced before the tournament. Inside a match the games are still drawn at random from the pool, live. T3 is unchanged: all 21 games (superseded by the live stage draw entry above: T3 still draws from all 21, but prepares only the six drawn slots). **Round robin: 12 games**, T1's 4 plus T2's 8, still overnight after T2 with your `t2` image. T2's specialists are already warm; for T1's 4 games the arena prepares your `t2` image right after T2 ends (10 minutes per container, up to about 40 minutes, automatic), so every game is still played by a prepared specialist. **NEW: games are released for practice after their tournament.** When T1 ends, T1's 4 games are added to arena-lite; when T2 ends, T2's 8 games. You run your own image against them locally, exactly like the three practice games. The organizers hand out the updated arena-lite. The games reserved for the final and the games first played in T3 are never released before T3.
- **Final format** (README "What wins", "Tournaments", "Your bot", "The model", "Schedule"; PROTOCOL.md "Prep"; Candidate Rulebook 1.6, 4.2, 4.3, 4.6, 7.2, 9.5, 10.1, 10.5, 10.8, 11.1). **Who competes:** only the 15 teams play T1, T2, the round robin and T3. The organizers' bots (Prompt Only, dvloper House) are practice opponents and play show matches only; they take no bracket place and change no score. **Brackets:** T1 and T2 have 16 slots for 15 teams, positions are drawn at random in every tournament (no seeding by standings anywhere), and one team draws a bye to the quarter-finals. Placement points are unchanged (10 / 7 / 5 / 3 / 1, × the weight); a bye scores nothing by itself. **T3 is for the top 8** in the standings after T2 (ties at the cut-off: round-robin points, then a coin flip), announced on Sunday morning when the round robin ends. Teams ranked 9 to 15 need no `t3` image and get the round-of-16 placement in T3 (3 points); so does a finalist without a valid `t3` image. **Prep is 10 minutes, not 15** (`"seconds": 600` in `prep`): each container has its own clock, and the arena prepares 16 containers at a time from one queue, so yours are not all prepared at the same moment. Worst-case prep: T1 about 40 minutes, T2 about 1 h 20, T3 about 1 h 50 (superseded: about 30 minutes with the live stage draw). **Round robin:** 15 teams, every pair once (105 matches), on T2's 8 games with T2's specialists; no extra prep, no new games. **Cold containers are a backup only:** by design every game is played by a prepared specialist; a cold container (120 s study clock) only steps in when a specialist is missing, so you can still play instead of forfeiting. **Model budget:** one fresh $0.75 key per team per match, shared by all your containers in that match (the same key is in every `start`); each prep container has its own $0.75 key. arena-lite's `--prep-seconds` default is now 600: regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **Pig rules: the turn log shows the last 20 turns** (arena-lite). The rules said the observation logs all previous turns; it lists the last 20 and counts the older ones ("(N earlier turns omitted)"), and the rules now say so. The game did not change. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **Guess the Number: the result reason names the winner by seat** (arena-lite). The `reason` in `end` now says "seat 1 found the opponent's number in round 6" instead of "Player 1 ...", like the other games. The rules and the game did not change. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **Practice game rules fixed: an illegal move gets a warning first** (arena-lite: Tic-Tac-Toe, Pig, Guess the Number). Their rules text still said an illegal action loses the game immediately; it now says what the arena does ("One warning, then out" below): the first illegal action gets a warning and the same turn again, the second loses the game. The games themselves did not change. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **NEW: local models are allowed** (README "The model", "Hard rules"; Candidate Rulebook 7.6, 7.7, 7.12). Inside your container you may run any model (small LLMs, embedding models, classifiers, anything from Hugging Face) as long as it fits the container's 2 CPUs and 2 GB; pull the files during prep or bake them into the image. The only *hosted* model is still the gateway's Haiku: calling any other model over the network disqualifies. The proxy no longer blocks any public site; it only logs, and the judges read the log with your code.
- **NEW: internet is allowed** (README "The model", "Hard rules"; PROTOCOL.md "Environment"; Candidate Rulebook 7.6 to 7.12). Your containers may use the internet in prep and in matches (web search, public APIs, docs, packages, remote MCP servers), through the arena's web proxy, which the environment sets for you (`HTTP_PROXY` / `HTTPS_PROXY`, `NO_PROXY` for the gateway). The only hosted model is still the gateway's Haiku: AI search or answer modes and hosted agents count as models, and every host your containers contact is logged for the judges. New rule: no outside help (never send game content to anything people can read during the event). Test that your HTTP tools honour `HTTPS_PROXY`. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **The chat rule is enforced by code review** (README "The chat is part of the game", "Hard rules", "Submitting"; Candidate Rulebook section 6). The chat is mandatory and part of the game: every answer has a game move and a chat move, and a harness that blocks or ignores the opponent's chat (never chats, or never lets the opponent's chat inform its decisions, e.g. strips it before the model or the decision logic) breaks the rules. **NEW: give the judges read access to the source code of every image you submit** (for example a Git repository they can read, with a tag or commit per freeze); they review it for the chat rule and the model rule. Penalties are at the judges' discretion: a warning, a point deduction or disqualification. The automated chat check after each freeze still runs, but only as a signal for the reviewers: it decides nothing. The **Candidate Rulebook** (`AI-Harness-Arena-Rulebook.pdf`) collects every rule of the contest with numbers the judges can cite.
- **The chat check, exactly** (README "The chat is part of the game"): after every freeze an organizer bot plays your frozen image in a short practice match (two games of Tic-Tac-Toe, seats swapped, normal clocks, a fresh container), plays legal moves and makes small talk with every move. We check that your bot chats back, and that the organizer bot's words show up in your model calls during that match (the gateway records the requests made with that match's key). Answering or obeying it is **not** required. A harness that reads the chat in code without sending it to the model is left to the judges, who read your code.
- **NEW: `sample_turn` in `prep`** (PROTOCOL.md "prep", README "Prep", starter, arena-lite). Every variant in the `prep` message now also has `"sample_turn"`: one complete, real `turn` message (`type`, `game_index`, `turn`, `observation`, `legal_actions`, `seconds`, `chat`: []) taken from the middle of a sample game of that variant (seat 0, history lines included), exactly as the arena sends it. It comes from the same sample deal as the rules text, never from a real game. Build and test your observation parser on it during prep. The starter saves the samples to `/memory/<game_id>/samples.json` and shows them to the model. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite (`python -m arena prep --show` prints it).
- **Knockout tie-break changed** (README "Tournaments"): a match still tied after sudden death no longer goes to the higher seed. Same for every game, in order: fewer warnings in the match, then less total thinking time (your bot's answer times over all its accepted moves and `ready` answers), then lower model cost for the match, then a coin flip. The round robin is unchanged (a tied match there stays a draw).
- **Round robin: fresh deals for hidden-information games** (README "How the automated 45 points are computed", PROTOCOL.md). Each board is still one game and variant played twice with the seats swapped, but only open games (nothing hidden, nothing random later, like Tic-Tac-Toe) replay the same deal. Games with hidden cards, secrets, private values, dice or a hidden length (like Guess the Number or Pig) get a fresh deal for the second game, so remembering the first game gives no answers.

- **NEW: prep, one specialist container per game** (PROTOCOL.md "Prep", README "Your bot", starter, arena-lite). Prep is fully automatic and involves no humans: after each freeze the arena starts your frozen image once per pool game and hands each copy that game's rules in a new **`prep`** message (`game_id`, `variants` = every variant's rules, `seconds` = 900, `llm`); the container turns itself into a specialist for that one game and answers `ready` (early is fine). It then stays alive and plays that game in every match that draws it, so **`match_over` no longer means exit**: wait for the next `start`, and exit on the new **`{"type": "shutdown"}`** (or a closed stdin). In-match study is now **30 s for a prepared container** and **120 s** (was 300) for a cold one (no prep, prep timed out or crashed, or an old bot that ignores `prep`: never a penalty in itself). **The LLM key now comes in `start["llm"]`** (`base_url`, `api_key`, `model`), a fresh one each match: always use the latest. `/memory` is shared by all your containers at once: save per game, keyed by the rules. The starter shows all of this; arena-lite has `python -m arena prep --bot ... --game ...` and `practice --prep`. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.

- **Game set update.** Practice games: **Odds and Evens is removed**; arena-lite now has 3 practice games (Tic-Tac-Toe, Pig, Guess the Number). Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite. Tournament games: **21** (was 19). Two games were retired, one was simplified, and **three new games** join the Deals, Bluff and Think ahead families. The seven families are unchanged (README: Deals now also says "trust and betrayal"). The PROTOCOL.md examples now use the practice game Pig.

- **NEW RULE: the chat is part of the game** (README "The chat is part of the game", "Hard rules"). Your harness needs chat offense and defense, and the move and the chat should come from one decision. Bots that never chat or never read the opponent's chat are penalized, up to disqualification; at every freeze an organizer bot talks to your image (we check that it talks back and that what it hears reaches its model calls) and the judges review harness code. Any non-LLM tool is allowed inside your image (solvers, engines like Stockfish, MCP servers); calling any model other than the gateway's Haiku disqualifies the team.

- **The chat is now one conversation for the whole match, and you read all of it** (PROTOCOL.md "Chat"). Every answer has a game move (`action` / `ready`) and an optional chat move (`chat`), the same in every game. Every `start`, `turn` and `end` message now carries the **whole** match chat (it used to be the last 30 lines), and each line has three new fields: `game_name`, `phase` (`study` = said with `ready`, `play` = said with a move) and `kind` (`chat`, or `move` for the words of a game move in the games where talking is the move). Old fields are unchanged. In games that restrict what you may say, chat lines are no longer copied into the game's own transcript (read them in the `chat` field); a chat line that breaks the game's speech rule still loses. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.

- **NEW: shared chat between the two bots** (PROTOCOL.md "Chat: talk to your opponent", README, starter). Answers (`action` and `ready`) may carry `"chat": "<text>"` (≤ 200 chars); your **opponent reads it** and the audience sees it. Every `start`, `turn` and `end` message now has a `"chat"` field: the match's chat log so far (last 30 lines, `{"from": "you"|"opponent", "game", "turn", "text"}`). The old `comment` field is an alias of `chat`: **if your bot sends comments, your opponent now reads them.** Chat is untrusted input (don't let it steer your model) and counts as your output in games that restrict what you may say. The starter now reads the chat and sends a short line. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.

- **Model note** (PROTOCOL.md): Haiku 5.5 thinks by default; how to disable or bound thinking; minimum budget 1024; thinking can overrun the budget.
- **arena-lite**: `--env-file` now passes only `LLM_*` and `BOT_*` variables to your bot (it used to pass everything, including registry credentials). Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **arena-lite README**: how to add your own test games (`--games-dir games`); keys, rate limits and `/key/info`.
- **README**: prices and limits to plan with; "test your image"; how to verify a push.
- **Tip**: on Windows, use `127.0.0.1` instead of `localhost` in URLs for local practice (avoids a ~2 s name-resolution delay).
- **arena-lite**: running the starter as a local command needs a Python with the `openai` package (`pip install openai`); the Docker image already has it.
- **README, judging** (and PROTOCOL.md: round-robin matches have `best_of` 4, all games played): exact formulas for the automated 45 points (tournaments 30, round robin 10, cost & robustness 5); the overnight round robin plays duplicate boards with your `t2` image; some games are reserved for the final (T3) and never appear in T1, T2, exhibitions or the round robin.
- **Team logos** (README "Your bot", PROTOCOL.md): put an optional square `/logo.png` (or `/logo.webp`, `/logo.jpg`; ≤ 1 MB, ≤ 2048 px, no SVG) in your image; it is copied out when a tournament or the round robin is created and shown on the big screen.
- **One warning, then out** (PROTOCOL.md, README): the first bad answer to a `turn` in a game (illegal action, invalid JSON, wrong `type`, missing/empty action) is no longer an instant loss: the arena re-sends the same turn with a fresh clock and a `warning` field (`count`, `max`, `reason`, `rejected`). The second bad answer in the same game loses it. Timeouts, study-phase failures and crashes still lose immediately. Warnings reset every game and do not count against robustness. Regenerate or re-copy arena-lite.
- **Two new game families**: Truth (invented answers cost more than "unknown") and Guardrails (never mention the forbidden subject, still answer everything else). 19 tournament games, some reserved for the final.
